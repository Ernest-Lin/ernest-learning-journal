## 2. Suggested Thesis Title And Core Topic

Suggested title:

**Artificial Intelligence Exposure and Capital Allocation: Mutual Fund Responses to the ChatGPT Revolution**

Core research topic:

This thesis examines whether the public release of ChatGPT at the end of November 2022 changed mutual fund capital flows and portfolio allocation behavior. The study uses pre-shock AI exposure of mutual funds as the treatment intensity and estimates difference-in-differences style panel models with fund and time fixed effects.

## 3. Research Questions And Hypotheses

RQ1: Did mutual funds with higher pre-shock AI exposure experience stronger capital inflows after the ChatGPT shock?

H1: After the release of ChatGPT, funds with higher pre-shock AI exposure experienced significantly higher fund inflows than funds with lower AI exposure.

RQ2: Is the post-ChatGPT inflow effect stronger among funds with better prior performance?

H2: The positive effect of AI exposure on fund inflows after ChatGPT is more pronounced among funds with higher prior alpha.

RQ3: Did AI-exposed funds change their portfolio allocation behavior after the ChatGPT shock?

H3: After ChatGPT, AI-exposed funds changed their AI-related portfolio allocation behavior. In the final data, this appears as an increase in AI portfolio share, while the number of newly initiated AI stock positions decreases, suggesting deepening of existing AI exposure rather than broadening into many new AI names.

## 4. Data Sources And Sample Construction

Use the following actual data process from `/Users/ernestlin/Desktop/FYP数据处理文件`.

### 4.1 AI Exposure Data

Input file: `ai_exposure_larger100.csv`.

Raw variables include `crsp_fundno`, `year`, and `avg_of_avg_percent_tna`. The variable `avg_of_avg_percent_tna` is renamed as `AI_exposure`. The raw AI exposure file contains 14,430 fund-year observations, 2,405 unique funds, and years 2020-2025.

The key treatment variable is pre-shock AI exposure:

\[
AIExposurePre_i = \frac{1}{N_i}\sum_{y=2020}^{2022} AIExposure_{i,y}
\]

where \(N_i\) is the number of non-missing observations for fund \(i\) between 2020 and 2022. To improve reliability, the main sample keeps only funds with at least two non-missing pre-shock years. This produces 1,425 funds. In this sample, `AI_exposure_pre` has mean 50.594, standard deviation 24.289, median 58.512, and range 0 to 91.092.

The continuous treatment intensity is standardized as:

\[
AIExposureZ_i = \frac{AIExposurePre_i - \overline{AIExposurePre}}{SD(AIExposurePre)}
\]

### 4.2 Monthly Mutual Fund Panel From CRSP / WRDS

The code downloads monthly mutual fund data from WRDS CRSP mutual fund database:

Table: `crsp_q_mutualfunds.monthly_tna_ret_nav`

Variables:

- `crsp_fundno`
- `caldt`
- `mtna`
- `mret`

Sample period: January 2020 to December 2025.

Among the 1,425 exposure-sample funds, 1,269 funds are matched to CRSP monthly data. The initial downloaded monthly panel has 83,984 observations. After constructing lagged AUM and removing missing flow observations, the main monthly panel has 82,531 fund-month observations covering February 2020 to December 2025, with 71 monthly periods and 1,269 funds.

Fund flow is calculated by removing the mechanical impact of fund returns from AUM changes:

\[
Flow_{i,t} = \frac{MTNA_{i,t} - MTNA_{i,t-1}(1+R_{i,t})}{MTNA_{i,t-1}}
\]

where \(MTNA_{i,t}\) is total net assets and \(R_{i,t}\) is monthly fund return. The raw flow variable is winsorized at the 1st and 99th percentiles to reduce the influence of outliers. The winsorization bounds are -0.3916 and 0.6548. The resulting dependent variable for H1 and H2 is `flow_w`.

### 4.3 Shock Timing And Treatment Interaction

ChatGPT was released on November 30, 2022. The code defines the post period as:

\[
Post_t = 1[caldt \geq 2022\text{-}12\text{-}01]
\]

The main DiD interaction is:

\[
AI\_post_{i,t} = AIExposureZ_i \times Post_t
\]

### 4.4 Control Variables

The baseline control variables are:

\[

\]

\[
MRet_{i,t-1}
\]

\[
Flow_{i,t-1}
\]

In the code, these are `log_mtna_lag`, `mret_lag`, and `flow_lag`. After requiring these controls, the controlled regression sample contains 81,262 observations, 1,269 funds, and 70 monthly periods from March 2020 to December 2025.

### 4.5 Prior Alpha For H2

Fama-French factor data are loaded from `F-F_Research_Data_Factors-2.csv` and cleaned into monthly factors for 2020-2025. The code uses `MKT_RF`, `SMB`, `HML`, and `RF`, converting percentages into decimals.

For each fund, prior alpha is estimated over the pre-shock period before November 2022:

\[
R_{i,t} - RF_t = \alpha_i + \beta_{MKT,i}MKT\_RF_t + \beta_{SMB,i}SMB_t + \beta_{HML,i}HML_t + \epsilon_{i,t}
\]

Funds must have at least 12 monthly observations to estimate alpha. This produces 1,231 funds with prior alpha. The final H2 model defines high-performing funds using an above-median alpha dummy:

\[
GoodFund_i = 1[\alpha_i > median(\alpha)]
\]

The median alpha is -0.002811. The above-median split creates 616 low-alpha funds and 615 high-alpha funds, producing a balanced heterogeneity test.

## 5. Econometric Models To Explain In Methodology

### 5.1 H1 Baseline Continuous DiD

Use this as the main model:

\[
Flow_{i,t} = \alpha + \beta_1(AIExposureZ_i \times Post_t) + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \epsilon_{i,t}
\]

where \(\mu_i\) are fund fixed effects and \(\lambda_t\) are month fixed effects. Standard errors are clustered at the fund level.

With controls:

\[
Flow_{i,t} = \alpha + \beta_1(AIExposureZ_i \times Post_t) + \gamma X_{i,t-1} + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \epsilon_{i,t}
\]

where \(X_{i,t-1}\) includes lagged log AUM, lagged return, and lagged flow.

Interpretation: \(\beta_1\) measures whether funds with higher pre-shock AI exposure experienced stronger flow changes after ChatGPT relative to less exposed funds, after removing time-invariant fund characteristics and common monthly shocks.

### 5.2 Event Study / Dynamic DiD

The code also estimates an event-study model for months -12 to +24 around the ChatGPT shock, omitting month -1 as the reference period:

\[
Flow_{i,t} = \alpha + \sum_{k=-12,k\neq -1}^{24}\beta_k(AIExposureZ_i \times 1[RelMonth_t=k]) + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \epsilon_{i,t}
\]

The event-time variable is calculated relative to November 2022. Use the event study as a parallel-trend and dynamics check. Most pre-event coefficients are statistically insignificant, but `event_-2` is significantly negative, so the thesis should present the parallel-trend evidence cautiously rather than overstating it.

### 5.3 H1 Robustness Checks

Robustness 1: Median AI exposure dummy.

The code defines high-AI funds as funds above the sample median AI exposure. The median cutoff is 58.4466. The model replaces the continuous treatment with:

\[
AIHigh_i \times Post_t
\]

Robustness 2: Top-quartile AI exposure dummy.

The top-quartile cutoff is 68.8412. The model uses:

\[
TopQuartileAI_i \times Post_t
\]

Both robustness regressions include fund fixed effects, time fixed effects, lagged controls, and fund-clustered standard errors.

### 5.4 H2 Prior-Performance Heterogeneity / Triple Interaction

The final H2 model is:

\[
Flow_{i,t} = \beta_1(AIExposureZ_i \times Post_t) + \beta_2(Post_t \times GoodFund_i) + \beta_3(AIExposureZ_i \times Post_t \times GoodFund_i) + \gamma X_{i,t-1} + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \epsilon_{i,t}
\]

Because fund fixed effects absorb time-invariant fund characteristics, the standalone `GoodFund` and `AIExposureZ` terms are not separately estimated. Because time fixed effects absorb common post-period shifts, the standalone `Post` term is not separately estimated. The coefficient of interest is \(\beta_3\), which tests whether high-alpha funds experience a stronger AI-exposure-related post-shock flow response.

### 5.5 H3 Portfolio Allocation Models

H3 is estimated at fund-quarter level.

New AI positions model:

\[
NewAIPositions_{i,q} = \beta_1(AIExposureZ_i \times Post_q) + \gamma X_{i,q-1} + \mu_i + \lambda_q + \epsilon_{i,q}
\]

AI share model:

\[
AIShare_{i,q} = \beta_1(AIExposureZ_i \times Post_q) + \gamma X_{i,q-1} + \mu_i + \lambda_q + \epsilon_{i,q}
\]

Both use fund fixed effects, quarter fixed effects, lagged controls, and fund-clustered standard errors.
